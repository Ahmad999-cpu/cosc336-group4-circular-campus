## 6. Financial Feasibility Analysis

### 6.1 Purpose and Assumptions

The financial analysis assesses whether the cost of developing and maintaining the Circular Campus system can be covered by the existing budget and whether the expected benefits outweigh the costs.

This includes development resources, infrastructure, AI services, ongoing maintenance, and savings from purchases that are no longer needed.

The project will begin with a student-developed prototype by four team members using existing computers.

No definitive project budget, supplier quotations, or university purchasing figures are currently available. Therefore, the amounts presented below are planning estimates only, expressed in AED (UAE dirhams). These estimates will require review once the technical approach and expected usage are more fully defined.

### 6.2 Estimated Prototype Costs

For budgeting purposes, the prototype is assumed to require three months of development and testing.

| Cost Category | Assumption | Estimated Cost |
|---------------|------------|----------------|
| Development tools and version control | Use suitable tools without paid licences and the required GitHub repository. | AED 0 |
| Computers and equipment | Use existing team computers; no dedicated hardware purchase. | AED 0 |
| Application hosting | Allow AED 50 per month for three months if remote hosting is needed. | AED 150 |
| Database, file storage, and backups | Allow AED 50 per month for three months. | AED 150 |
| AI services | Allow AED 100 per month for classification, matching, assistant features, and reporting. | AED 300 |
| Testing and demonstration | Use existing equipment, team testing, and sample data. | AED 0 |
| **Subtotal** | | **AED 600** |
| Contingency | Reserve 20% for unexpected usage or additional services. | AED 120 |
| **Total Estimated Prototype Cash Budget** | | **AED 720** |

These amounts are budget allowances rather than supplier prices.

Hosting, database, and storage services may be bundled together, so the team must avoid counting the same service twice. Local or university-provided infrastructure could also reduce direct spending.

Student development time does not require salary payments within this course project, but it remains a resource cost.

The team should record time spent on:

- Requirements analysis
- Development
- Testing
- Documentation

A professional deployment would require a separate labour estimate based on actual working hours and approved rates.

### 6.3 Operating and Long-Term Maintenance Costs

After development, the system would require continued spending and staff effort to remain reliable and useful.

| Recurring Cost | Description |
|----------------|-------------|
| Hosting and data storage | Running the application and storing asset records, photographs, documents, and backups. |
| AI usage | Processing classifications, matches, questions, and generated reports. |
| Software maintenance | Fixing defects, updating dependencies, and maintaining integrations. |
| Technical support and administration | Managing accounts, resolving user issues, monitoring services, and checking backups. |
| Data and AI quality review | Correcting asset information and evaluating recommendation quality. |
| Training | Introducing users to the platform and updating guidance when workflows change. |

Using the prototype allowances, hosting, storage, and AI services would total approximately:

- **Monthly operating cost:** AED 200
- **Annual operating cost:** AED 2,400

These figures assume that usage remains similar to the prototype.

This is only an illustrative service-cost baseline. It excludes staff labour and does not represent a complete production operating budget.

Before campus deployment, the university would need to estimate:

- Number of users
- Volume of assets
- AI service demand
- Technical support hours
- Security requirements

### 6.4 Expected Financial Benefits

The main financial benefit of Circular Campus is avoiding unnecessary purchases by transferring suitable existing assets to departments that need them.

Repair and refurbishment may also delay replacement purchases.

Other potential benefits include:

- Reduced time spent locating resources
- Better utilisation of existing equipment
- Lower storage costs
- Reduced disposal expenses

These benefits should only be assigned monetary values when supporting evidence is available.

#### Net Reuse Benefit Calculation

For each qualifying reuse transaction:

**Net Reuse Benefit = Avoided Purchase Cost - Inspection, Repair, and Transfer Costs**

For example, assume a department would otherwise purchase an equivalent computer for AED 2,000.

If preparing and transferring an available computer costs AED 300, the estimated net reuse benefit would be:

```text
Net Reuse Benefit = AED 2,000 - AED 300
                  = AED 1,700
```

This is an illustrative example.

A transfer should only count as an avoided purchase when it replaces a genuine planned purchase and meets the department's requirements.

The original purchase price of the reused asset should not automatically be treated as the amount saved.

### 6.5 Cost-Benefit and Break-Even Analysis

The following scenarios illustrate whether reuse benefits could cover the estimated AED 720 prototype cash budget.

| Scenario | Qualifying Reused Items | Average Net Benefit per Item | Total Net Reuse Benefit | Benefit After Prototype Cash Cost |
|----------|-------------------------|------------------------------|-------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Low Benefit | 2 | AED 200 | AED 400 | -AED 320 |
| Moderate Benefit | 5 | AED 300 | AED 1,500 | AED 780 |
| High Benefit | 10 | AED 500 | AED 5,000 | AED 4,280 |

These scenarios are not forecasts.

They demonstrate how financial feasibility depends on:

- The number of purchases genuinely avoided
- The costs required to make reused assets suitable
- The average financial benefit generated per reuse transaction

#### Break-Even Calculation

If the average net benefit is AED 300 per qualifying item:

**Break-Even Quantity = Total Prototype Cash Cost / Average Net Benefit per Item**

```text
Break-Even Quantity = AED 720 / AED 300
                    = 2.4 items
```

Since only complete items can be reused, the break-even quantity must be rounded up.

**Break-Even Quantity = 3 Qualifying Items**

Therefore, three qualifying reused items would cover the prototype's estimated direct cash cost.

However, this does not establish full project profitability because student labour and future operating costs are excluded.

For a campus deployment, total net reuse benefits over a defined period would need to exceed implementation, operating, maintenance, and support costs over the same period.

### 6.6 Financial Risks and Cost Controls

| Financial Risk | Proposed Control |
|----------------|------------------|
| AI spending exceeds the allowance. | Set usage limits, monitor spending, and retain basic search functionality when AI services are unavailable. |
| Hosting and storage costs grow with usage. | Monitor capacity, limit upload sizes, and review service plans before expanding. |
| Repair or transfer costs outweigh avoided purchases. | Compare expected costs and asset suitability before approving reuse. |
| Low participation produces limited savings. | Start with a small departmental pilot and measure completed exchanges. |
| Savings are overstated or counted twice. | Verify avoided purchases and record costs against individual transactions. |
| Maintenance effort is underestimated. | Record support and maintenance hours during the pilot and include them in future budgets. |

### 6.7 Financial Feasibility Conclusion

The Circular Campus prototype appears financially feasible under the proposed assumptions because it uses existing equipment and has an estimated direct cash requirement of **AED 720**.

Its potential financial value comes primarily from avoiding unnecessary purchases and extending the useful life of existing assets.

The team recommends proceeding with a controlled prototype while tracking actual spending and reuse outcomes.

A wider campus deployment should depend on:

- A validated implementation and operating budget
- Evidence of genuine avoided purchases
- Measured reuse benefits
- Realistic infrastructure and maintenance estimates
- Confirmation that financial benefits can cover ongoing operating and support costs

Overall, Circular Campus offers a potentially cost-effective approach to improving university resource utilisation while supporting long-term financial and environmental sustainability.

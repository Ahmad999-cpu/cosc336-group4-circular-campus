6. Financial Feasibility Analysis
6.1 Purpose and Assumptions
The finance analysis assesses what the cost and opportunity of developing and maintaining the Circular Campus system can be delivered from the existing budget and whether the benefits outweigh the costs. This includes development resources, infrastructure, AI services, ongoing maintenance, and savings from purchased items that are no longer needed.
The project will begin with a student developed prototype by four of the team members on existing computers. No definitive project budget or supplier quotations or university purchasing figures are available at present. As such the amounts given below are planning estimates only, in AED (UAE dirhams), and will require review once the technical approach and total estimated usage are more fully defined.

6.4 Expected Financial Benefits
The main financial benefit is avoiding unnecessary purchases by transferring suitable existing assets to departments that need them. Repair and refurbishment may also delay replacement purchases.
Other potential benefits include less time spent locating resources, better use of existing equipment, and lower storage or disposal costs. These benefits should only be assigned monetary values when supporting evidence is available.
For each qualifying reuse transaction:
Net reuse benefit = avoided purchase cost − inspection, repair, and transfer costs
For example, assume a department would otherwise purchase an equivalent computer for AED 2,000. If preparing and transferring an available computer costs AED 300, the estimated net reuse benefit is:
AED 2,000 − AED 300 = AED 1,700
This is an illustrative example. A transfer should only count as an avoided purchase when it replaces a genuine planned purchase and meets the department’s requirements. The original purchase price of the reused asset should not automatically be treated as the amount saved.

Therefore, three qualifying items would cover the prototype’s estimated direct cash cost. This does not establish full project profitability because student labour and future operating costs are excluded.
For a campus deployment, total net reuse benefits over a defined period would need to exceed implementation, operating, maintenance, and support costs over that same period.

6.7 Financial Feasibility Conclusion
The Circular Campus prototype appears financially feasible under the proposed assumptions because it uses existing equipment and has an estimated direct cash requirement of AED 720. Its potential value comes primarily from avoiding purchases and extending the useful life of existing assets.
The team recommends proceeding with a controlled prototype while tracking actual spending and reuse outcomes. A wider deployment should depend on a validated budget and evidence that financial benefits can cover ongoing infrastructure, maintenance, and support costs.
